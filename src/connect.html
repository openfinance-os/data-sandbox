Connect

How AI assistants reach your bank data

Three shapes a connection can take in (or alongside) UAE Open Finance. All three return the same v2.1 data contract — only the consent journey varies. Two are tryable below; the third is shown for context as the pattern UAE Open Finance was designed to replace.

Nothing here is endorsed by Anthropic, CBUAE, Nebras, or any UAE LFI. Every persona is synthetic; bank profiles are anonymous Rich/Median/Sparse populate-rate bands, never real UAE bank names (PRD NG5 / D-14).

For context · what UAE Open Finance replaced

Off-rails aggregator (Plaid-pattern) Not compliant in UAE

⚠ Screen-scraping
User hands their internet-banking credentials to an aggregator that logs in as them and reads the screen. The bank never sees an authorization step.
⚠ Partner back-channel
Aggregator and bank arrange a back-channel feed via a commercial deal. Less risky than scraping, but no consent broker — the user has no central registry to inspect or revoke.
✓ Al Tareq (Journey 2)
User authorizes a TPP through Al Tareq's CAAP. Consent Manager is the single source of truth. Every LFI sees a regulated request; the user has one place to inspect and revoke.

In markets without open finance, multi-institution aggregation works the way Plaid built it in the US: customers paste their bank credentials into a third party that logs in as them, or a commercial partner deal opens a back-channel feed. It works — and it's how a lot of consumer fintech got off the ground — but the user has no consent broker, no scope picker, and no central revocation portal.

UAE Open Finance is built to close that gap. Article 15 of CBUAE Circular C 03/2025 is explicit:

Licensees must […] establish dedicated interface via API Hub for secure access. […] [The following are] prohibited: data scraping or similar extraction activities; interception of digital connections via reverse engineering.

That's why Journey 1 is not Journey 3, even though both bypass a third-party consent broker. J1 is a bank serving its own customer with its own data — not "Data Sharing" in the regulated sense, so Article 15's prohibitions don't apply. J3 is a third party reaching across institutions without the regulated rails. That's the line UAE OF draws.

How to revoke (J1)

Because J1's consent is the bank's own OAuth — not Al Tareq — revocation lives in the bank's labs portal, not the regulator's Consent Manager. Locally, the equivalent is DELETE /mcp with Mcp-Session-Id — the sandbox-mcp server destroys the session and the next tool call gets a 401.

In a real LFI deployment, this would be a "Connected apps" / "Active sessions" page in the bank's mobile or web app, with one-click stop sharing per connector. The UAE OF Consent Manager is not in this loop — that's J2's territory.
